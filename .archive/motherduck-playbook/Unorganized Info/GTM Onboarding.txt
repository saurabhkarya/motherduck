Details
Introduction to FY27 Plan (10X) Someone in Space Needle introduced the FY27 plan, branded as "10X," noting that their fiscal year starts in February and ends in January. This plan was developed during a corporate offsite in the fall and pitched to the board in December. Ryan Boyd requested that someone in Space Needle cover the 10X material, as someone in Space Needle is local.
The Four Pillars of the 10X Plan The 10X plan is structured around four pillars that are oriented like a customer journey: Land, Ramp, Mature, and Scale. Someone in Space Needle explained that Land aims to acquire 75 customers per month, involving projects such as B3 pricing and fixing the trial flow sign-up process. Ramp focuses on accelerating customers to production faster, ideally ensuring new sales customers generate a minimum of 6K in annual revenue and accelerating their progress within the first 60 days.
Mature and Scale Pillars Someone in Space Needle detailed the Mature pillar, which focuses on adding features often considered "table stakes," such as SSO, and expanding their role as a cloud data warehouse to be competitive, like the launch of the EU region. The goal for Mature is to close 100 customers with more than 500 employees. The Scale pillar is designed to address concerns about scaling and data capacity, aiming to increase annual contract value from $6k-$24k to $40k-$100k plus, and target customers with over 100 terabytes of data. Scott Mendelson emphasized that these are highly ambitious goals, far beyond the company's current standing, which will push the team to achieve new capabilities.
10X Plan Leadership and Future Integration Someone in Space Needle identified the leaders for each pillar: Scott Mendelson and Garrett for Land, someone in Space Needle and Norris for Ramp, Francis and Sheila for Mature, and Ryan Boyd and Boaz for Scale. Ryan Boyd mentioned that the 10X plan has not yet incorporated recent learnings related to the MCP server and AI work from the previous four to six weeks, but they will find a way to integrate them. Someone in Space Needle also noted that Caitlyn will be leading the effort to meet the 10X goals.
Go-to-Market Overview and Customer Lifecycle Ryan Boyd presented an overview of the go-to-market structure and the customer lifecycle, featuring Scott Mendelson and someone in Space Needle. Ryan Boyd explained the progression of leads, starting with Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs)—those who sign up for the product, download content, or attend events. The BDRs then work to contact these MQLs and convert them into Sales Accepted leads through meetings, eventually qualifying them as Sales Qualified Leads (SQLs) if they represent a good use case.
Sales and Customer Teams' Collaboration Ryan Boyd described how the Sales team (AEs) and someone in Space Needle's organization, the hatchery team (Customer Engineers or CEs), work together to convert qualified leads into customers, aiming increasingly for annual contracts, which can be consumption-based or based on reserved capacity. Following conversion, the goal is for customers to get fully ramped, expand into new use cases, and continue their engagement over time, aiming for longer than the market average of seven years for a data warehouse customer. Someone in Space Needle added that the ultimate goal is for customers to become enthusiastic referrals and champions in the community.
Marketing Strategy and Evolution Ryan Boyd outlined the two main areas of marketing: core marketing and DevRel. DevRel, which includes advocates and a new Developer Experience engineer named Dumpkey, focuses on improving the online customer experience through documentation and tutorials. Ryan Boyd reviewed the evolution of marketing messages, from "making analytics fun, frictionless, and ducking awesome" to "infrastructure for answers," which incorporates AI, LLMs, and the MCP server to provide business answers.
Sales Team Philosophy and Ambition Scott Mendelson, the head of sales, acknowledged the strong brand and the consumption-based business model, which dictates a customer-first mindset. Scott Mendelson expressed the highly ambitious goal of building the best sales and BDR team that any infrastructure or technology business has seen, emphasizing that they are setting out to do hard things and excel as the underdog in a contested market. Scott Mendelson invited anyone with questions about pitching, positioning, or comparison to competitors like Snowflake to reach out directly.
The Hatchery Team (Customer Success) Someone in Space Needle, head of Hatchery Success, explained that the team's mission is to create a customer-first culture focused on helping customers win in their workloads, potentially securing their next promotion. The hatchery team includes Customer Engineers (led by Mike Shu), who partner closely with the Sales AEs to bridge the expertise gap for customers, and the Support team (led by Olivia), which currently provides 12-hour coverage and plans to expand to 18 hours. Someone in Space Needle emphasized that the team's human strength lies in problem-solving and empathy, distinguishing their approach from transactional support that could be handled by AI.
Partnerships and Conclusion Ryan Boyd confirmed that the go-to-market strategy is inclusive of the partnership organization, which assists with co-marketing and closing joint deals. Scott Mendelson ended by congratulating the group for being the first to see the presentation deck.
