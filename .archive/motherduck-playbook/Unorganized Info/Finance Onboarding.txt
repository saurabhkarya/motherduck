Finance Team Structure and Resources Someone in Space Needle introduced the finance team, including Evan, the controller, whose scope includes payroll, staff accounting, and finance operations. They noted that the finance team is actively interviewing for an FP\&A (Financial Planning and Analysis) lead and sought recommendations for candidates. Someone in Space Needle highlighted important resources for new employees, such as the finance notion site, which contains information on vendor onboarding, corporate credit card policy and expensing processes (Expensify or Remote for Europe), and purchasing non-software and software items.
Payroll and Financial Policy Overview Someone in Space Needle clarified the payroll schedule, noting that the US employees are paid semi-monthly (1st–15th and 16th–end of the month), unlike the typical every-other-Friday structure, while Europe-based employees are paid monthly. Hourly employees are limited to a bi-weekly basis. They explained that the finance function is evolving from simply paying bills to becoming a strategic partner, driving decisions, and helping plan for future features by providing clear visibility into the health of the business.
Annualized Weekly Revenue (AWR) Performance and Goals Someone in Space Needle discussed the company's financial performance, focusing on AWR, which stands for Annualized Weekly Revenue, a metric chosen because it closely reflects how customers use the compute business. The initial AWR target was $2 million, which the company quickly surpassed, leading the leadership team to double the goal. Someone in Space Needle noted that while the goals are ambitious and hard to hit, the projected growth for the year—estimated to land at about $3.3 million—is significant and something the team should be proud of.
Liquidity, Fundraising, and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) Someone in Space Needle outlined key financial metrics, including cash on hand (liquidity), average burn rate, and projected cash out date, which inform future fundraising discussions, noting that the board is very excited about the company's trajectory. In response to a question about CAC, they indicated it is currently higher than desired, as marketing efforts are heavily tied to events which can be sensitive, but that the company has a strategic focus, known as "10x," aimed at optimizing this. This focus includes a "land pillar" to acquire more customers by concentrating events in key areas like San Francisco and exploring new customer acquisition methods, such as sponsoring independent studies.
Impact of DuckDB 1.4 Rollout on Revenue A speaker inquired about a gap in the revenue goal, which someone in Space Needle attributed to the rollout of DuckDB 1.4. This release made the product more efficient and cheaper for customers, which was a "headwind" to that month's revenue goal but is beneficial for long-term customer satisfaction and retention because customers prefer cheaper and faster services. Someone in Space Needle emphasized that the company will always prioritize the customer, choosing to deploy features that benefit them even if it impacts short-term revenue goals.
Shift to Input Metrics and Business Flywheel Someone in Space Needle discussed shifting focus from output goals, such as pure revenue targets, to input metrics that translate better to individual roles. These input metrics include measuring content production, customer utilization of MotherDuck, feature launch cadence, and qualified marketing leads, as successful execution on these inputs is expected to result in subsequent revenue growth. They introduced the business flywheel, explaining that the engineering work that customers love drives their increased consumption, which attracts partners, leading to economies of scale. The resulting excess investment can either be reinvested into product development or used to lower the cost structure for customers.
Decision-Making in Ambiguous Situations and Scaling Finance Regarding how finance analyzes ambiguous consumption-based products, someone in Space Needle suggested a focus on understanding desired outcomes, testing assumptions with data, and providing scenario-planned ranges for ROI (Return on Investment). They stressed that in a startup environment, optimizing for speed is crucial, especially when competitors are likely making similar decisions. Someone in Space Needle concluded by explaining that the finance team must prioritize capital allocation across different pillars like marketing and engineering with varying ROI expectations. They noted that the finance function is scaling slowly compared to builder functions like engineering and go-to-market teams to ensure continued investment in building and growth.
