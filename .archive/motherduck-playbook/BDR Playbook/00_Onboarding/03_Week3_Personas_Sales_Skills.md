## Days 1-2: Know Your Audience

**Goal**: Understand who buys MotherDuck and why.

### Our Two Primary Audiences

| Audience | Focus | Characteristics |
|----------|-------|-----------------|
| **Data Engineers** | Data Warehouse work | Building pipelines, infrastructure |
| **Software Engineers / Product** | Applications | Building products, customer-facing |

### Understanding Engineers

Engineers share common characteristics:
- Can be skeptical and flakey
- Work in bursts
- May schedule calls and ghost
- Get pulled into urgent issues
- Disappear while heads-down in projects

**Your Approach:**
- Read the room - know when to push, pause, or provide resources quietly
- Build trust by listening and asking thoughtful questions
- Show you understand their world
- Pay attention and know your stuff!

> You can't fake understanding with this audience. Take time to learn technical concepts, workflows, and pain points that matter to them. Engineers respect someone who has done the homework.

**Most Importantly**: Have fun! People buy from people who make them feel good. Get on the same side of the table.

### Core Personas

| Persona | Day-to-Day | Cares About | Common Objections |
|---------|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Data Engineer** | Building/maintaining pipelines | Reliability, simplicity | "We already have Snowflake" |

## Days 3-4: Discovery & Qualification

**Goal**: Learn to ask the right questions and identify good-fit prospects.

### The Discovery Mindset

Discovery is **hunting for clues**, not giving a sales pitch. Be disarming, loose, and fun to get the answers you need.

> Don't position or pitch until you have a sense of what they're thinking about and why.

Engineers are unpredictable. Your discovery is your safety net, your map, and your guide through the deal.

**Key Principles:**
- Read the signals and adapt
- Stay curious
- Be direct about what matters
- Be open
- Don't make assumptions

### Core Skills

| Skill | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Running effective discovery | Uncover real needs |
| Qualification frameworks | Identify good fits |
| Identifying pain points | Map to MotherDuck solutions |
| Knowing when to schedule CE/AE | Proper handoff timing |

## Day 5: The Sales Cycle & Handoff Process

**Goal**: Understand the full customer journey and your role in it.

### Why Process Matters

Buyers don't evaluate solutions for fun. They evaluate to solve real problems. An organized sales cycle gives them:
- Clarity
- Confidence
- Structure

Our role is to provide that structure, polish, and guidance.

> A disciplined process helps us focus on customers with real commercial intent, so our energy goes where it matters most.
