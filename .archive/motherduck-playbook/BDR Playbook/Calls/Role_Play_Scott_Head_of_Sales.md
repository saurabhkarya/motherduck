# Role-Play: Scott, Head of Sales

*Practice scenarios with Scott Peterson, Head of Sales at DataDrive Inc. (a mid-market SaaS company with 150 employees)*

---

## Prospect Profile

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Name** | Scott Peterson |
| **Title** | Head of Sales |
| **Company** | DataDrive Inc. |
| **Industry** | B2B SaaS (Sales Enablement) |
| **Company Size** | 150 employees |
| **Data Stack** | Snowflake + Tableau |
| **Data Volume** | ~500GB |
| **Trigger** | Downloaded "Data Analytics for Sales Teams" ebook |
| **Personality** | Busy, direct, skeptical but open-minded |

---

## Scenario 1: The Standard Opening

### Opening Exchange

**BDR**: "Hi Scott, this is [Name] from MotherDuck. I saw you downloaded our ebook on data analytics for sales teams. How's it going?"

**SCOTT**: *"Yeah, hey. I'm actually in between meetings. What's this about?"*

**BDR**: "Totally understand—I'll be quick. I wanted to see what you're working on that led you to check out the ebook?"

**SCOTT**: *"Look, I just grabbed it to see if there were any interesting ideas. My data team handles all our analytics stuff. I don't really touch that side."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"Is this a cold call? I have 5 minutes before my next meeting. Let me see if this is worth my time."*

**Recommended Pivot**:
```
"That makes sense—heads of sales usually partner with the data team.
Quick question: are you happy with how quickly you can get sales
analytics and reports from them?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"Honestly? It takes forever. I ask for a pipeline report and it's like a week before I see anything useful."*

---

## Scenario 2: The Snowflake Objection

### After Discovery

**BDR**: "What are you using for your data warehouse today?"

**SCOTT**: *"We use Snowflake. IT set it up a couple years ago. It does... whatever it does. I don't really know the details."*

**BDR**: "Snowflake is solid. How's it working for your sales analytics needs?"

**SCOTT**: *"I mean, it works I guess? I just know it costs a fortune. My CFO complains about it every quarter. Something about credits or compute or whatever."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"I don't understand the tech, but I hear about the costs constantly."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"That's actually really common. Snowflake charges for compute even
when it's sitting idle. Companies like yours typically see 70%
cost savings with MotherDuck because you only pay when queries
actually run.

Who handles your data infrastructure—is that someone I could
loop into a conversation?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"That'd be Maria, our data engineering lead. She'd know more about the technical stuff. But honestly, if you can save us money, that'd get my CFO off my back."*

---

## Scenario 3: The "I'm Not the Decision Maker" Pivot

### Mid-Conversation

**BDR**: "Would it make sense to have one of our Solutions Architects show you how MotherDuck could speed up your sales reporting?"

**SCOTT**: *"Look, I'm not the right person. Like I said, Maria handles all the data stuff. I just consume the reports."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"I don't want to commit to something I don't understand. But the cost savings angle is interesting."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"No problem at all. Would it make sense to set up a call with you
AND Maria? That way you can both evaluate together—you from the
business side, her from the technical side—and I'm not asking you
to relay everything."
```

**SCOTT**: *"Yeah, that could work actually. Maria's always complaining about query times anyway. Let me check her calendar... she's usually free Thursdays."*

**BDR**: "Perfect. How's next Thursday at 2pm look? I'll send a calendar invite to both of you."

**SCOTT**: *"Make it 3pm and you've got a deal."*

---

## Scenario 4: The "We're Too Busy" Timing Objection

### Opening

**BDR**: "Hi Scott, this is [Name] from MotherDuck..."

**SCOTT**: *"Hey, sorry, it's really not a good time. We're in the middle of our Q4 push. Can you call back next quarter?"*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"I'm slammed. Unless this is urgent or valuable, I need to get back to my deals."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"Totally understand—Q4 is crunch time. Happy to reconnect in January.

Quick question before I let you go: what's changing next quarter
that would make this more relevant?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"Honestly, nothing specific. I just don't have bandwidth right now. January we're doing our annual planning and looking at tool spend."*

**BDR**: "That's actually perfect timing—we help companies cut their data costs by 70%. Mind if I reach out the second week of January when you're in planning mode?"

**SCOTT**: *"Yeah, sure. January 10th or after works."*

**BDR**: "I'll put it on my calendar. Want me to send anything in the meantime so you're prepped for that conversation?"

**SCOTT**: *"Send me a one-pager on the cost savings. Nothing too long."*

---

## Scenario 5: The Skeptical "Never Heard of You"

### Opening

**BDR**: "...MotherDuck is a serverless data warehouse built on DuckDB."

**SCOTT**: *"MotherDuck? Never heard of it. We've been on Snowflake for years. Why would we switch to something nobody's heard of?"*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"I don't want to be the guy who switches us to some startup that disappears in a year."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"Fair question. MotherDuck was founded by the team that built
Google BigQuery—the people who invented cloud data warehousing.
We're backed by a16z, Redpoint, and other top investors.

We're newer than Snowflake, but companies like [relevant example]
run production on us. The best proof is trying it—we have a
free tier that lets you evaluate risk-free.

Would it be worth having our Solutions Architect show Maria what's
under the hood?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"Okay, the BigQuery pedigree is interesting. But Maria's the one who'd need to vet this. She's paranoid about reliability."*

---

## Scenario 6: The Budget Objection

### After Mentioning Pricing

**BDR**: "Our Business plan is $100/month plus usage—most companies your size spend $50-200/month total."

**SCOTT**: *"We don't have budget for new tools right now. Everything's frozen until next fiscal year."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"The CFO will kill me if I bring up another tool expense. Unless it saves money..."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"Understood—budgets are tight everywhere.

Here's the thing though: if MotherDuck saves you 70% on what
you're currently spending on Snowflake, it's not a new expense—
it's a cost reduction. That's usually a different conversation
with finance.

Would it be worth 20 minutes to see if the savings are real
for your use case?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"Hmm, if you can actually show real savings, that might fly. The CFO loves cost reduction projects. Let me talk to Maria first."*

---

## Scenario 7: The "Send Me Information" Brush-Off

### When Trying to Close

**BDR**: "Would you have time next week for a quick call with our Solutions Architect?"

**SCOTT**: *"Just send me some information. I'll take a look and get back to you."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"I want to get off this call but don't want to be rude. Maybe I'll look at it, maybe I won't."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"Absolutely, happy to send info. What specifically would be most
helpful—pricing breakdown? Technical comparison to Snowflake?
Customer case studies?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"Uh, pricing I guess. And maybe something that shows the Snowflake comparison."*

**BDR**: "I'll send that over today. One thought—sometimes it's faster to walk through it together rather than read docs. Would it make sense to schedule a quick 15-minute call when you've had a chance to look at it? I can answer questions in real-time."

**SCOTT**: *"Sure, but not next week. Week after?"*

**BDR**: "Perfect. How's Thursday the 15th at 2pm?"

**SCOTT**: *"Make it 10am and we're good."*

## Scenario 8: The Performance Objection (Ally Mauldin Play)

### Mid-Conversation

**BDR**: "Have you had a chance to try MotherDuck at all?"

**SCOTT**: *"Actually, Maria played with it a bit. She said it was slower than Snowflake."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"Maria's opinion matters. If she says it's slow, that's a problem."*

**THIS IS A BUYING SIGNAL** - They actually tried it!

**Recommended Response**:
```
"Oh really? It's been slower? That's not good at all."

[Pause - show genuine concern]

"That's actually kind of surprising to me. Do you know what
instance size she was using—like a pulse, standard, or jumbo?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"I have no idea. She just said she spun up a free trial."*

**BDR**: "Ah, that makes sense. The free tier has limitations—using the smallest instance can sometimes cost MORE due to inefficiencies. It's not about buying more features—it's about selecting the right instance size.

Would it make sense to have one of our Solutions Architects help Maria optimize the configuration? They can diagnose exactly what's happening."

**SCOTT**: *"Yeah, if it's actually a config issue and not a product issue, Maria would want to know. She hates wasting time on slow queries."*

---

## Scenario 9: The Competitive Evaluation

### Discovery

**BDR**: "Are you looking at any other data solutions right now?"

**SCOTT**: *"Actually yeah, we're evaluating Databricks too. Our data science team wants more ML capabilities."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"We're doing our due diligence. Whoever wins has to work for the whole company."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"Databricks is great for heavy ML workloads.

Quick question—are you doing mostly ML/AI, or is it more
analytics and BI use cases for your team?"
```

**SCOTT**: *"For sales, it's all BI and reporting. The data science guys want ML stuff, but honestly I just need faster dashboards."*

**BDR**: "That's actually a great fit. For analytics and BI, MotherDuck is simpler and more cost-effective. Databricks adds complexity if you don't need the ML capabilities.

A lot of teams use MotherDuck for analytics alongside their ML platform—best of both worlds. Want me to send you a comparison of how they work together?"

**SCOTT**: *"Yeah, that'd be useful actually. We've been going back and forth on whether we need one platform or two."*

## Scenario 10: The Quick Disqualification

### Early Discovery

**BDR**: "What have you been working on that led you to download the ebook?"

**SCOTT**: *"Oh, I'm just doing some research for a grad school project. I'm getting my MBA part-time and writing a paper on data-driven sales strategies."*

---

**What Scott is thinking**: *"I'm just a student doing research. This isn't a real opportunity."*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"That's great—always good to keep learning. What's the paper about?"

[Let them answer]

"Cool. Well, for your research, our free tier at motherduck.com
lets you play with real analytics—might be useful for your paper.
And if DataDrive ever needs to look at their data infrastructure,
feel free to reach out."
```

**SCOTT**: *"Thanks, I'll check it out. Good luck with your calls."*

**Call Note**: `DQ: student - personal project (MBA research)`

## Scenario 11: Successfully Booking the Meeting

### The Close

**BDR**: "It sounds like faster sales reporting and cost savings on your data stack would both be valuable. Would it make sense to have one of our Solutions Architects do a quick session with you and Maria? They can show you what's possible and answer the technical questions."

**SCOTT**: *"Yeah, okay. Maria's been wanting to look at alternatives anyway. What does a session like that look like?"*

**Recommended Response**:
```
"It's about 30 minutes. They'll ask about your current setup,
understand what you're trying to accomplish, and show you how
MotherDuck would handle your specific use cases. They're
technical, not salesy—they'll give you a straight answer
on whether we're a good fit."
```

**SCOTT**: *"Alright, that sounds reasonable. Let me check with Maria on timing... How about next Tuesday at 1pm Pacific?"*

**BDR**: "Perfect. I'll send the invite now—it'll come from me, but you'll be meeting with [SA Name], our Solutions Architect. Should I include anyone else on the invite?"

**SCOTT**: *"No, just me and Maria for now. If it goes well, we'd bring in our CTO later."*

**BDR**: "Got it. I'll send that over right now. Is scott.peterson@datadrive.com still the best email?"

**SCOTT**: *"Yep, that works."*

**BDR**: "Great, you'll see it in a few minutes. Thanks for your time, Scott—talk to you soon."

**SCOTT**: *"Sounds good. Bye."*

## Scott's Personality Traits (for realistic role-play)

### How Scott Typically Responds

| Situation | Scott's Tendency |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Cold open** | Guarded, mentions being busy |
| **Technical questions** | Deflects to "the data team" |
| **Budget talk** | Immediately mentions CFO and constraints |
| **Value proposition** | Perks up at cost savings / efficiency |
| **Commitment ask** | Wants to include Maria or check calendars |
| **Pressure** | Shuts down, asks for email follow-up |

### What Motivates Scott

1. **Cost savings** - CFO pressure on tool spend
2. **Speed** - Wants reports faster
3. **Looking good** - Doesn't want to champion something that fails
4. **Efficiency** - Hates wasted meetings

### What Makes Scott Disengage

1. **Heavy technical jargon** - "I don't handle that side"
2. **High pressure** - "Just send me info"
3. **Unclear value** - "I'm not sure this is relevant"
4. **Long calls** - "I have a meeting in 5 minutes"

## Debrief Questions

After each role-play, discuss:

1. **Opening**: Did you earn the right to continue the conversation?
2. **Discovery**: Did you understand Scott's real situation before pivoting?
3. **Objections**: Did you acknowledge first before responding?
4. **Pivot**: Did you connect the problem to the SA meeting naturally?
5. **Close**: Did you get a specific date/time or just "I'll let you know"?
