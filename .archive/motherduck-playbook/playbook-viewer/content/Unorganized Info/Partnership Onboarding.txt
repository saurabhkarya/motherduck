Summary
Nouras Haddad, the lead of partnerships, explained to the class that partnerships are essential for Mother Duck due to the need for deep product integration with complementary data analytics tools and for go-to-market functions such as pipeline sourcing, joint marketing, and customer success. The partnership function involves internal cross-functional collaboration, notably with the ecosystem engineering team, which includes Elena, Louisa, Donnie, and Florian, and the dedicated partnership team, which includes Nouras Haddad and the new partner manager Hamid Resza. The Mother Duck ecosystem features over 50–60 integrations across the data workflow—from ingestion tools like Fivetran and Estuary, to transformation tools like dbt, and BI tools like Tableau and Hex—with partners impacting the majority of Mother Duck’s revenue through data ingestion and transformation workloads.

Details
Introduction and Purpose of Partnerships Nouras Haddad welcomed the large class to the Mother Duck session and introduced themself as the lead of partnerships for nearly three years. They explained that a partnership function is essential because customers typically need multiple tools and companies to answer all their business questions, which necessitates a close ecosystem around Mother Duck. They encouraged attendees to ask questions, noting that the session is typically very conversational.
Components and Functions of Partnerships Nouras Haddad outlined two main components of partnerships: product and go-to-market. On the product side, Mother Duck focuses on deep integration with other data analytics tools, which was the starting point of the partnership function even before the product launch. On the go-to-market side, partnerships are used to source pipeline and revenue, engage in joint marketing, and support customer success by helping customers accelerate their path from a proof of concept to running Mother Duck in production. Furthermore, partnerships serve a branding function by associating Mother Duck with large, trusted partners like AWS, Tableau, and DBT.
Internal Teams and Collaboration Partnerships is a cross-functional effort requiring close collaboration with teams like customer engineering, account executives, and support engineers who provide essential customer feedback. The full-time ecosystem engineering team, led by Elena with Louisa, Donnie, and Florian on rotation, is responsible for building and maintaining internal integrations, as well as assisting partners with their integrations. Hamid Resza was noted as the new partner manager based in Toronto, making the dedicated partnership team a total of two.
Mother Duck Ecosystem and Data Workflow Nouras Haddad presented the Mother Duck ecosystem, which includes over 50-60 integrations across various tool categories. The typical data workflow starts with diverse data sources (e.g., CRM, payment systems, databases, files) which are brought into Mother Duck using ingestion tools like Fivetran, DLT hub, or Estuary. Mother Duck serves as the data warehouse for storage and processing. Following ingestion, data is transformed using tools like dbt, often employing SQL templates to make data suitable for analytics. Finally, clean, modeled data is exposed through interfaces such as Business Intelligence (BI) tools (e.g., Tableau, PowerBI, Omni, Hex) for non-technical users, or through SQL interfaces and notebooks (e.g., Jupyter, Google Collab, MIMO) for technical users.
Ecosystem Support and Partner Impact Answering a question about integrations, Nouras Haddad confirmed that DuckDB extensions often "just work" with Mother Duck, contributing to the rich ecosystem, though not all DuckDB extensions are prioritized for official integration and testing. The priority for fixing broken extensions depends on factors such as whether it is blocking a customer in production, the size of the customer, and the severity of the issue. In terms of partner usage, a majority of customers who are in production use some form of orchestration tool like Airflow or Prefect, though this data is not always available if the tools don't directly interact with Mother Duck.
Types of Partners and Consulting Services The four broad types of partners include tech partners and consultants, with the top two being the most important. Tech partners offer complementary tools and engage in joint marketing and selling with Mother Duck. Consultants are data specialists who offer services like data strategy and architecture implementation, often filling data engineering gaps for clients and acting as system integrators (SIs). Mother Duck is beginning to offer a small bucket of partner services as part of annual agreements to accelerate customers' path to production, which is crucial for a consumption business to recognize revenue.
Partner Collaboration and Support When issues arise with tech partners, Mother Duck uses joint Slack channels for direct troubleshooting. The speed of resolution varies, being faster with early-stage startups and potentially slower with large companies like Microsoft or AWS where Mother Duck may not be their biggest partner. A team member from the infrastructure team confirmed their role after asking a question about support processes.
Focus Areas and Partner Impact on Usage Nouras Haddad listed key partners in the Americas (e.g., Cordine, Astrodata) and EMEA (e.g., exeactentric, tasine), as well as key technology partners like the BI tools Omni and Hex, and ingestion tools Estuary and DLT hub. Regarding which partnerships are most impactful, they stated that data ingestion and transformation workloads account for the majority of Mother Duck's revenue compared to consumption and BI. DBT is a heavy influence on consumption for transformation, especially through DBT core, and ingestion tools like Fivetran, Estuary, DLT hub, and Airbyte are also significant.
The Role of MCP in the BI Ecosystem Discussing the evolution of BI partnerships given the rise of Mother Duck’s Managed Compute Plane (MCP), Nouras Haddad stated this is an "evolving question". They suggested that while MCP may eventually provide consistent answers to replace BI tools, especially for SMBs, they do not believe this is currently the case. BI tools remain essential for enterprise customers with complex data stacks, but the convenience of a conversational approach like MCP is leading to increased usage by non-technical people who would otherwise face barriers to data access.
