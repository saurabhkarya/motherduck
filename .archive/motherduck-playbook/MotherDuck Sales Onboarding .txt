Days 3-4: Deep Dive on DuckDB & MotherDuck
Goal: Understand what makes DuckDB special and how MotherDuck extends it

Notes: DuckDB wasn’t named because it sounds cute or quirky. It literally comes from the Hannes’ pet duck named Wilbur. The duck ended up inspiring the mascot and branding around how versatile and resilient DuckDB is.

DuckDB began as a research project at CWI in the Netherlands, where the team realized there was a missing quadrant in database tech. An analytical (OLAP) engine that runs inside a process without needing a big server infrastructure. They started building at DB as simple as SQLite but built for big, analytical workloads

Prototyping began in 2018, and by 2021 they were ready to spin it out. Today, DuckDB racks up millions of downloads per month from users all over the world, including brands like Tesla, Apple, and others Garrett O'Brien

Core Concepts:
- DuckDB basics: embedded OLAP, columnar engine, single-node architecture
- Why DuckDB is so fast (vectorized execution, columnar format)
- MotherDuck's dual execution model
- Scale-up vs scale-out philosophy
- Hypertenancy and cloud orchestration

Week 3: Customer Personas & Sales Skills
Days 1-2: Know Your Audience
Goal: Understand who buys MotherDuck and why.

Note: We sell to two primary audiences. Data Engineers working on a Data Warehouse, and Software Engineers, Product, working on a product or Application. 

Each has distinct areas of focus, concern, skill sets. 

However, they share similar characteristics. Engineers can be skeptical, flakey, and prone to working in bursts. They may schedule a call and ghost, get pulled into urgent issues, or disappear for hours or even days while heads-down in a project. 

Reading the room is critical: know when to push, when to pause, and when to provide resources quietly. Build trust by breaking through and listening, asking thoughtful questions, and showing that you understand their world. 

Pay attention and know your shit!  

You can’t fake understanding with this audience. Take the time to learn the technical concepts, workflows, and pain points that matter to them. Engineers respect someone who has clearly done the homework: know the stack, understand data pipelines, and think like they do. 

Lastly, have fun! Read your audience, engage them, relax, and let it go. People buy from people who make them feel good. This is not to say you shouldn’t challenge, push, and drive the deal. But, get on the same side of that table.


Core Concepts:

Days 3-4: Discovery & Qualification
Goal: Learn to ask the right questions and identify good-fit prospects

Note: Discovery is hunting for clues, not giving a sales pitch. Be disarming, loose, and fun to get the answers you need. 

It’s about asking smart questions, listening harder than you talk, and figuring out what actually matters to the person in front of you. Don’t position or pitch until you have a sense of what they are thinking about and why. 

Engineers and technical buyers are unpredictable. They work in bursts, get pulled into fires, and sometimes disappear for a few days. Your discovery is your safety net, it is your map, and will be your guide through the deal. 

Your job is to read the signals, adapt, and stay curious.
Be direct about what you think matters, be open, and don’t make assumptions.
