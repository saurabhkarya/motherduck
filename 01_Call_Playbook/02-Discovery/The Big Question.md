# The Big Question
**Start your discovery here - but remember: Give and Get**

---

## The Framework: Give → Leave Open → Ask

Every exchange should follow this pattern:
1. **Give** something helpful (answer their question)
2. **Leave something open** (allude to something interesting)
3. **Ask** an open-ended question (get them talking)

> "You need to give a little bit to get a little bit."

---

## What You Need to Find Out

1. **What are you working on?** ← The job to be done
2. **What are you currently using?** ← The tools
3. **Where's my hook?** ← Where we have home field advantage

---

## Opening Question

**Don't be too direct.** Earn the right to ask.

```
"I'd love to learn a bit about what you're working on -
what brought you to explore MotherDuck?"
```

Or after explaining what MotherDuck is:
```
"Maybe it'd be helpful if I understood a little bit about
how you're approaching your data strategy today?"
```

---

## Alternative Openers (Open-Ended Only)

**GOOD (open-ended):**
- "Tell me a little about your data stack - what are you using today?"
- "What types of things are you powering with that?"
- "What's on your mind from a data strategy perspective?"

**BAD (yes/no - kills the call):**
- "Is cost a concern?" → "No." → Dead.
- "Are you happy with Snowflake?" → "Yes." → Dead.
- "Is that something you're thinking about?" → "No." → Dead.

---

## Finding Your Hook

As they talk, listen for where you can position MotherDuck with **home field advantage**:

| They Mention | Your Hook | Why We Win |
|--------------|-----------|------------|
| External-facing apps, customer dashboards | **External analytics** | Per-query concurrency, no over-scaling |
| Lots of users hitting reports | **Concurrency** | Isolated compute per query |
| Using Claude, Cursor, AI workflows | **Agentic / MCP** | First-class AI integration |
| Small team, startup, SMB | **Cost** | They feel bad spending on Snowflake |
| Gold tier, serving layer, data products | **Serving layer** | Built for this use case |

---

## When They Mention a Competitor

**Don't attack.** Ask about their use cases instead.

```
Them: "We use Snowflake today."

You: "Cool - tell me a little about that. What types of things
     are you using Snowflake for? What are you powering with it?"
```

Then listen for your hook.

---

## If They're Happy With Their Current Setup

**Don't force cost/speed.** Find another hook.

```
"Yeah, it sounds like Snowflake's working well for the core warehouse.

A lot of customers use us as their gold tier or for external-facing
analytics - do you do anything like that today? Any customer-facing
dashboards or apps that need data?"
```

Put them where you want them. → [[00-Start/Core Principles#Find the Hook]]

---

## The Judo Move (When You're On Your Back Foot)

If they're being difficult or you feel defensive:

```
"Yeah, I mean, we definitely see different use cases...
Maybe I misunderstand a bit - what's on your mind from a
data strategy perspective? What types of things are you exploring?"
```

Flip it back to them. Be charming and aloof.

- [ ] **Their Hook:** Cost / External / Agentic / Serving layer / Speed

## When to Book the Meeting

Once they're interested or asking good questions → **STOP. Book the meeting.**

```
"Let's do this - how's your calendar look later this week?
We can set up some time, go through MotherDuck,
and answer any questions you have."
```

Don't over-close. You're just here for the meeting.

---

## Key Reminders

- [ ] No yes/no questions

- [ ] Don't pitch until you know their situation
- [ ] Where's my hook?
