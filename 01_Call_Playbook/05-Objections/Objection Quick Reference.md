# Objection Quick Reference
**Scan during calls. Stay relaxed. Flip back to a question.**

---

## The Framework
**Don't defend. Don't argue. Give → Leave open → Ask**

When you're on your back foot:
1. Give a relaxed, aloof answer
2. Leave something intriguing open
3. Ask an open-ended question back

> "Make it feel like you're answering the question, then flip back to a really good question. It's like Judo."

---

## Competitor Objections

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|
| "We use Snowflake" | "Cool - tell me a bit about that. What are you using Snowflake for today? What types of things are you powering?" |
| "We use BigQuery" | "Cool - what types of workloads are you running on it?" |
| "We use Databricks" | "Interesting - is it mostly ML workloads or analytics too?" |
| "Snowflake works fine for us" | "Yeah, sounds like it's working well for the core warehouse. A lot of customers use us alongside for gold tier or external-facing stuff - do you do any of that?" |

**Key insight:** Don't attack competitors directly - it triggers defenses. Ask about their use cases, then find your hook.

---

## "We're Happy With What We Have"

**Don't force cost/speed if they don't care.** Find another hook.

```
"Yeah, it sounds like [competitor] is working well for the core stuff.
A lot of people see an immediate application for us for things like
external-facing analytics or agentic analytics...

I'm not sure - are you thinking about any of that, or what's on your
mind from a data strategy perspective?"
```

## Timing Objections

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|
| "Not looking right now" | "Totally get it. What types of things would you be working on when the timing makes sense?" |
| "It's too early" | "Fair - what would 'ready' look like for you?" |
| "Check back next quarter" | "What's changing next quarter?" |
| "Just signed a contract" | "When does it renew? Happy to follow up before then." |
| "I'm busy" | "Totally fair - better time later this week?" |

---

## Interest Objections

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|
| "Just send me info" | "What specifically would be helpful - pricing, technical docs, customer stories? I'll send exactly that." |
| "We don't need this" | "Got it. What types of data challenges are you working through right now?" |
| "Not interested" | "Totally fair. Before I let you go - what would need to change for this to be worth revisiting?" |

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|

| "Data is too small" | "Perfect - no minimums. What are you using it for?" |

## Effort Objections

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|
| "Migration sounds painful" | "Most teams run us alongside first - no big migration, just a parallel test." |
| "Don't have time" | "That's actually why - serverless, nothing to manage. Running queries in an hour." |
| "We don't have budget" | "Free tier has 10GB and 10 hours. Worth exploring what it could do?" |

## When You're Stuck

**The Judo Move:**
```
"Yeah, we definitely see different use cases...

Maybe it'd be helpful if I understood a bit more about how you're
approaching things today? What's on your mind from a data perspective?"
```

**The "Lead the Witness" Move:**
```
"A lot of people see an immediate application for things like
external-facing analytics or agentic use cases - worried about
the load they'd put on their warehouse...

I'm not sure, are you thinking about any of that?"
```

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't ask yes/no questions** - "Is cost a concern?" kills the call
- **Don't pitch before you know their situation** - you'll box yourself in
- **Don't defend MotherDuck** - flip it back to them
- **Don't attack competitors directly** - ask about use cases instead

---

## After Handling Objection

Once they're engaged or asking questions → Book the meeting

```
"Let's do this - how's your calendar look later this week?
We can set up time and go through MotherDuck together."
```
